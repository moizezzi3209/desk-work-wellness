Desk Work Wellness

Understand your working week.

A simple personal reflection on total sitting across your day, including desk work and other activities, and the movement that interrupts it. Record what happens, notice your patterns and choose one achievable change.

This is an awareness tool, not a fitness tracker or health-risk assessment. Record your sitting during desk work and your other sitting during the rest of the day. Your estimated total daily sitting is the sum of those two figures. Do not include sleep.
Your entries save automatically. Use the same device and browser each day. Before clearing browser data or moving to another device, use “Export my entries” below to keep a copy. Private/incognito browsing may not retain entries after you close it.

1. Record my week

Use estimates. You do not need to measure every minute.

Seven days

My daily sitting tracker

For each day, record two separate estimates: (1) sitting during desk work, such as computer work, reading, writing, studying, paperwork and seated meetings; and (2) other sitting outside desk-work time, such as commuting, meals, television or streaming, reading, socialising and other seated activities. The calculator adds them to show your estimated total sitting time. Count each period once only.

How the daily total is worked out: Desk-work sitting + other sitting = estimated total sitting for that day. Select “No desk work” on a day without desk work; this allows the calculator to count work sitting as zero while still including other sitting. Leave “Not recorded” when you have not entered the work-day status. Leave other sitting blank if you have not estimated it; enter 0 only if there was none.

Your best estimate in hours, including sitting outside work. Enter 0 only if you had no uninterrupted sitting period.

My daily movement log

For each day with desk work, record movement that actually interrupted or replaced desk-work sitting. Each occasion should be counted once. Leave fields blank if you have not recorded them; enter 0 when there were none.

Which box should I use? If getting up is part of an activity you were already doing, record it as everyday/task-related movement. If you deliberately pause desk work to move, record it as a desk-work movement break. Choose the closest description; do not overthink the reason. The same type of activity, such as walking or stretching, may fit either box depending on the occasion.

3. Explore a change

Small opportunities to replace some sitting with movement can be part of a working day.

Ideas to consider
Between tasksWalk for a few minutes after completing a task, where practical.
During suitable callsStand or walk during a phone call when it is safe and appropriate.
Between meetingsUse the transition to move rather than remain seated, when possible.
At a natural pauseStretch, change position or take a short movement break.

There is no rigid hourly break prescription here. Choose opportunities that fit your work, environment and individual circumstances.
